Date: 27/08/2025

## Overview

Assembling the complete set-up and running preliminary tests to verify temperature performance

## What worked?

* I used M3 × 16 screws (without washers for now) to secure the copper plate, top plate, and heat sink together.
*  I connected the Peltier cells in series for testing.
* The fans (0.4 A at 12 V) were powered using Power Supply B. They cannot be connected to Power Supply A, since the Peltier setup requires 32 V, while the fans are limited to 12 V.
* The fans are connected in parallel and appeared to work properly during the initial run.

## TEST RUN:

* Adding a thin layer of alcohol on the copper plate stopped ice from forming.
* The plate cooled steadily down to –11.2 °C in 10 mins.
* Bringing in the extra metal fans helped a bit — the plate dropped further to –13.3 °C after 2 mins.

## Issues Identified

* I realised that I cannot connect the Peltier cells in parallel, even with the second power supply (Power Supply B), due to its voltage/current limitations:
    * 6 V / 5 A → insufficient, since each Peltier can draw up to 6 A at 16 V.
    * 30 V / 1 A → also unsuitable, as the current is too low.

## TEST RUN:

* Cooling stopped at –11.2 °C with the first setup, so the system hit a limit there.
* Heat sinks were sitting at ~32 °C, pointing to not enough airflow from the fans.
* Even after adding the metal fans, the plate plateaued again at –13.3 °C, so airflow seems to not be the big problem here.

## Next steps:

* Try to add washers to the setup for better stability
* Test if improving thermal paste application between layers would make a bigger difference
* Consider adding some insulation during the testing to reduce heat gain from the surroundings.
