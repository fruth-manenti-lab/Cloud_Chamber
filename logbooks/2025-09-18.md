# Date: 18/09/2025

## Purpose:

Test cooling performance of the 150 × 150 × 2 mm copper plate using the newly delivered NTC thermistor to verify previous FLIR inconsistencies and measure cold-side temperature drop reliably. 
Goal: establish FLIR inconsistencies and explore if copper plate surface area affects minimum temperature achieved.

## Experimental Setup:

* Peltier cells: 2 × TEC2-19006 in series
* Copper plate: 150 × 150 × 2 mm
* Power supply: 28.45 V, 5.2 A (below safe limits)
* Temperature measurement: NTC thermistor (accuracy ±0.1°C, backlash precision ±0.1°C, high-temp protection)
* Procedure: Applied a thin layer of alcohol on the copper plate to prevent ice formation. Water vapour from the chamber can condense and freeze on the plate surface, releasing latent heat and slightly increasing the plate temperature. The alcohol layer prevents ice formation, reducing unwanted heat input.
* Limitations: Only one measurement point above a single Peltier cell; cannot capture full plate temperature distribution.

## Analysis:

* FLIR “specific temp” roughly tracks thermistor but fluctuates and differs in magnitude.
* Possible causes of FLIR inconsistency:
    * Alcohol layer affects emissivity
    * Rapid cooling phases — Camera unable to show decrease in temperature in real time 
    * Reflections from surrounding materials — Highly plausible
* Conclusion: Use NTC thermistor to get precise measurement

## Observations (Qualitative):

* Thermistor probe attachment was difficult due to alcohol layer.
* Rapid cooling observed in first few minutes.

## Potential Issues Identified & Hypotheses:

1. Limited thermal contact: Uneven thermal paste or insufficient clamping may reduce cooling efficiency.
2. Temperature distribution unknown: Only one measurement point → edges are warmer.
3. Plateau around –15 °C: Cold-side may be limited by copper mass or Peltier efficiency.
    * Hypothesis: reducing plate surface area could decrease thermal mass and allow lower minimum temperature.
