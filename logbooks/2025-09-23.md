# Date: 23/09/2025

## Purpose:

To replace the 150 × 150 × 2 mm copper plate with a 150 × 40 × 2 mm plate and test whether the smaller plate size improves the cooling rate and helps reach the desired temperature of –30 °C.

## Experimental Setup:

* Copper plate: 150 × 40 × 2 mm 
* Peltier cells: 2 × TEC2-19006 connected in series
* Power supply: 32 V, 5.2 A
* Thermistor: NTC type, attached to the copper plate using tape
* Thermal paste: Reapplied between copper plate, top plate, and heat sink
* Mounting: Used screws and nuts to clamp the copper plate, top plate, and heat sink together (the screw-and-nut method — need to confirm proper term later)
* Surface treatment: Applied a thin layer of alcohol on the copper plate to prevent frost/ice formation. This prevents the water vapour from transferring latent heat and forming ice, keeping the surface liquid for stable thermistor contact.

## Procedure:

* Drilled holes into the smaller copper plate. The hole placement was misaligned, so the holes had to be made larger.
* As a result, the M3 screws were now too small to clamp the plate securely against the Peltier cells and top plate.
* Disassembled the setup and reapplied thermal paste.
* Attempted to fasten the system using only screws, but they kept slipping out due to the enlarged holes.
* Alex suggested using nuts to secure the screws in place. Initially, this was avoided to prevent  any possible electrical conduction between the copper plate and heat sink, but since the screws were metallic, adding nuts made no difference.

Used the screw-and-nut method to clamp the plates together. The copper plate was now significantly flatter against the Peltier cells, with minimal visible gaps.

## Observations:

* The overall setup remained the same apart from the smaller copper plate.
* Voltage drawn: 28.3 V, Current drawn: 5.2 A
* The TEC2-19006 Peltier cells are rated to work up to 6 A, but according to Curious Scientist (their graph attached below), they perform most efficiently at around 5 A to 5.5 A. Increasing the current above this point increases Joule heating and decreases net cooling.

## Cooling rate observation:

The plate reached 0 °C in 2 minutes from room temperature and –28 °C in around 10 minutes, which is significantly faster than the previous setup.

## Analysis and Discussion:

* The improvement could be due to both the new clamping method (screw + nut) providing better contact between the Peltier cell and the copper plate and the smaller plate having lower mass and surface area, resulting in faster cooling.
* Danish mentioned Fourier’s Law of Cooling, and we discussed how the larger copper plate might not have been able to reach –30 °C due to limitations in the cooling power available relative to its surface area.
* This confirmed the idea that testing thinner plates would be exciting and confirm if mass has an effect on cooling rate.
* Achieving a temperature near –30 °C indicated a strong improvement in thermal performance.

## Additional Test:

To check if alcohol vapour condensation could be observed, we placed a plastic container on top of the plate and sealed with blue tack, but no visible alcohol vapour or particle trails were seen — could be due to potential gaps that are causing the alcohol vapour to leak.
