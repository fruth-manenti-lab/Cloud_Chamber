## Experimental Setup:

* The Peltier cells (2 × TEC2-19006) were connected in series.
* The glass chamber was placed on top of the copper plate prior to starting measurements (some visible gaps at the interface).
* Voltage: 30.46 V
* Current drawn: 5.60 A
* The rest of the setup remained consistent with previous runs.
### Copper Plate Preparation:
* The 1 mm copper plate was cut to match the base dimensions of the glass chamber
* Edges were sanded for safety and handling
* Holes were drilled in a more structured manner for better alignment
### Assembly:
* Thermal paste was reapplied before assembly
* The copper plate, top plate, and heat sinks were clamped using the screw-and-nut method
* Minor visible gaps remained, but the contact was significantly improved compared to the 2 mm plate

## Observations:

* The cooling rate improved notably with the thinner copper plate.
* The plate reached –25 °C in approximately 11 minutes.
* Alcohol vapours were observed fogging and forming droplets near the edges of the chamber, but no supersaturated vapour layer was visible—likely due to leakage in the system.
* Having the chamber covering the copper plate appeared to improve the cooling rate, likely because it reduced air convection over the plate surface.
* Upon removing the chamber, the temperature rose from –25 °C to –22 °C, and then took ~4 minutes to return to –25 °C. This suggests that sealing the chamber could further lower the equilibrium temperature.

## Analysis / Reflection:

* The thinner copper plate improved cooling efficiency due to reduced thermal mass, allowing faster temperature drop.
* Thermal contact and electrical stability were consistent throughout the run.
* Leakage remains a key issue preventing the formation of supersaturated vapours and visible particle tracks.
* The results confirm that plate thickness strongly influences cooling rate and final steady-state temperature.
